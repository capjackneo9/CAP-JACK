# What can NCVR TV air? (cartoons, music videos, commercials)

*General guidance, not legal advice. Before airing anything you didn't create, confirm with an entertainment attorney.*

## The short answer on classic cartoons
| Example | Can NCVR air it? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Tom and Jerry** (1940 →) | ❌ Not without a license | Still under copyright (MGM, now Warner Bros. Discovery). |
| **"Hammerman"** — the MC Hammer cartoon (1991, DIC) | ❌ Not without a license | Under copyright. The rights passed with DIC's library, now WildBrain, and MC Hammer's name and likeness are separately protected. |
| **Your own cartoons** | ✅ Yes | You own them. This is the best path. Keep written agreements with any animators, voice actors and composers. |
| Cartoons published **1930 or earlier** in the US | ✅ Generally yes | US works published in 1930 or earlier are public domain as of Jan 1, 2026 (e.g. *Steamboat Willie*, 1928). |
| Some cartoons from the 1930s–50s | ⚠️ Title-by-title | Some shorts fell into the public domain because their copyright was never renewed. Commonly cited examples are several Fleischer *Superman* shorts and some *Popeye*, *Betty Boop* and *Looney Tunes* shorts. Each one must be checked. |

## Rules of thumb
- **Paying, crediting, or "no copyright intended" does not make it legal.** Fair use does not cover airing full episodes.
- **"Old" doesn't mean free.** Most cartoons from the 1940s onward are still owned by studios that enforce their rights.
- **Public-domain films can still carry traps:**
  - Restored or colorized versions, and new soundtracks, can have their own new copyright.
  - Characters can be **trademarks** (Mickey Mouse, for example). Don't use them in your branding or imply a studio endorses NCVR.
- **Verify public domain** with the US Copyright Office renewal records, or use collections that document their research (e.g. Internet Archive). Keep notes of where each file came from.
- **To air a classic legally, license it.** Contact the rights holder or a distributor, e.g. WildBrain for DIC titles or Warner Bros. Discovery for Tom and Jerry. Get the territory (worldwide streaming), term and price in writing.

## Your own cartoons — protect them
- Make sure everyone who works on them signs a **work-for-hire or assignment agreement** that gives NCVR (or Caprio Jackson) the rights.
- **Music in the cartoon** needs rights too. Use your own catalog or licensed production music.
- Register important episodes with the US Copyright Office.

## Saturday Morning Cartoons and kids
- If the block is aimed at children, keep commercials kid-appropriate. Follow the BBB National Programs **CARU** guidelines for advertising to kids, and don't collect personal data from children under 13 (FTC **COPPA**).
- You can turn **commercial breaks off** for any TV schedule block in Studio → NCVR TV.

## Music videos and commercials
- **Music videos:** you need rights to both the song and the video. Your own videos are fine. For guest artists, get written permission covering streaming on NCVR TV.
- **Commercials:** the advertiser confirms they own everything in the spot (music, footage, logos). NCVR labels each spot "Advertisement" on screen.
